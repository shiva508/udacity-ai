# AI Agents and Agentic Workflows
## The Modern AI Agent: A System with a "Brain"
_Modern AI agents use a Large Language Models (LLMs) as their central processing unit, or 'brain'. This is what allows agents to understand goals, reason about complex situations, and make decisions._

_But it's more than just a brain. A true agent is a complete system designed to:_

* **Perceive** its environment.
* **Reason** about what it observes.
* **Formulate** plans to achieve its goals.
* **Execute** actions to carry out those plans.

_This ability to perceive, reason, plan, and act with a high degree of autonomy is what separates these agents from simple automation._

## Agentic Workflow or Simple Automation?
_Where do we draw the line between an agentic workflow and standard automation?_

_If a system just follows a series of sequential, fixed steps, we're talking about automation, not an agentic workflow. A perfect example is the automated email drip campaign:_

1. A user subscribes to a newsletter.
2. The system automatically sends a pre-written "Welcome Email."
3. After 3 days, it automatically sends a pre-written "Follow-up Email."

_This is a classic deterministic workflow. It's efficient for its one specific task, but it cannot adapt or make decisions._

_So, what's the difference in an agent-based system? Instead of having prescribed steps to execute, the agents must decide which steps to take, and how to execute the details of each distinct process they are presented._
